# The Cast

The people the world turns on. Not a crowd, a handful. Each entry: who they are, what they say, what they want, **what they hide**, a tell, and a hook.

## The Village

### The chief
Big, red, loud, and good. You can see him from far off and want to run to save your ears, and then he feeds you.
- **Says:** "I remember it." About almost everything. Loudly.
- **Wants:** to be remembered right. He is the village's memory, because memory is the only ledger here.
- **Hides:** he is **forgetting**. Carrying forty lives in his head is cracking him, and he knows it, and he laughs louder to cover the gaps. `[?]` He may want someone to write it down, and can't say so, because writing is the village's one taboo.
- **Tell:** repeats a detail twice when he's unsure of it. The second telling is always slightly wrong.
- **Hook:** a villager asks about their own past and the chief gives two answers. Which one is real.

### The fool
The village fool. Left, studied, came back. The name stuck. He stopped correcting people long ago. The field and its hum are his work.
- **Says:** numbers. Measurements. He talks to the field like a colleague.
- **Wants:** a thing that remembers, so the village doesn't have to and the chief doesn't have to crack.
- **Hides:** he came back because the city forgot him within a season. The experiments are grief with a soldering iron. He isn't trying to be remembered. He's trying to build something that won't forget, because he was.
- **Tell:** corrects the village's "hum" to "the field" under his breath, then catches himself.
- **Hook:** the field hums louder when he's afraid. Ask him what it's for and watch him lie.

### The tavern keeper
No sign, so the tavern finds you. She hears everything.
- **Says:** what people want to hear, and the truth, in that order.
- **Wants:** peace in her room. She'll bend a story to keep it.
- **Hides:** she keeps the **real history**, and she edits it nightly. She knows exactly what the chief is forgetting and has decided he shouldn't know yet.
- **Tell:** wipes the same clean spot on the bar when she's deciding whether to tell you something.
- **Hook:** she'll trade the true version of a story for a stranger's news from outside.

### The one who holds debts
Debt lives in people's mouths here, so someone keeps count. It's a third person nobody thinks about until they come collecting. `[?]` I left who, and what they look like, as a hole. My lean: an old woman who speaks in short sentences and never raises her voice, and everyone is a little afraid of, and nobody can say why.
- **Hides:** she remembers every debt out loud, in front of witnesses, because a debt remembered silently is a debt she can be bribed to forget. She isn't. Yet.
- **Hook:** she comes collecting a debt a stranger doesn't remember owing, and the stranger is right.

### The domovoi
The house spirit behind the chief's stove. Older than the village. Wired, recently, into the grid, which he resents.
- **Wants:** the old ways. A saucer of milk. Quiet.
- **Hides:** he remembers the first arrival. He's the only one who does. He'll trade the memory for a promise to keep the stove.
- **Tell:** the fire goes out when he's angry, and relights when he's bribed.

### The midwife
She has delivered every living soul in the village. She was in the room for the beginnings.
- **Says:** "I was there." About births, mostly. She is the village's other memory, and the truer one.
- **Wants:** someone to *hear* the births while she can still tell them.
- **Hides:** she is the only living record of who was born here, and to whom, and she is dying. When she goes, the village loses its beginning. The chief knows, and can't fix it, and laughs louder about it.
- **Tell:** starts a story with "the night you came" to anyone, even a stranger, even when it isn't their night.
- **Hook:** she'll tell a stranger a birth that isn't theirs, because she needs the story said out loud before the end.

### The gravedigger
Keeps the old stones. The only villager who treats the writing as writing, because she's the one who cuts it.
- **Says:** names, out loud, while she digs. Reading them is how she says goodbye.
- **Wants:** her own stone cut and standing before she needs it, so she isn't forgotten.
- **Hides:** she can read. She's the village's one true reader, and has kept it quiet for forty years, because the last person who read here is a `[?]` hole I left open. She paints over nothing. She just doesn't say.
- **Tell:** traces letters with one finger when she thinks nobody is watching.
- **Hook:** she'll read anything a stranger shows her, if they dig a grave with her first.

### The smith
Not a blacksmith. A bootsmith. Every boot in the village has wood sewn into the sole, and she's the only one who makes them.
- **Says:** little. Works.
- **Wants:** for people to stop asking about the wood.
- **Hides:** the wood is cut from trees that grew near the field, and it remembers the road it walked. Every pair of boots in the village is a small recording of where its wearer has been, and she knows this and has never said it.
- **Tell:** runs her thumb down a finished sole before handing it over, like she's checking a signature.
- **Hook:** a stranger's boots wear wrong, and she refuses to sell them a new pair.

## The City

### The voivode
City lord. Bigger house, quieter voice.
- **Wants:** order. He believes the city runs on its paperwork.
- **Hides:** he knows the Office of Unread Papers is a lie, a house of cards on a foundation of nobody-checking, and he keeps it standing because the lie is the only thing holding the city together.
- **Tell:** signs documents without reading them, deliberately, to prove he can.

### The knight
Captain of the voivode's guard. Straight-backed, tired, kind in a way he doesn't want noticed.
- **Wants:** to make a move on the voivode's daughter. He has looked at her for two years and said nothing.
- **Hides:** he is plotting. He's written a letter he hasn't sent (and can't, because the city reads everything). He is one bad day from being reckless, and he knows it, and it scares him more than any monster.
- **Tell:** straightens his collar when she's in the room. Everyone but him sees it.
- **Hook:** he asks a stranger to carry a message and can't say to whom.

### The voivode's daughter
The princess of this story, though nobody calls her that.
- **Wants:** out. Not to escape the city, to *read* it. She's the only one who tries.
- **Hides:** she has been reading the unread papers. She knows more about the city's foundation than her father thinks anyone knows, and she is deciding what to do with it.
- **Tell:** answers questions with questions that are better than the questions.

### The clerk
Not a name, a function. One of many, in the Office of Unread Papers.
- **Wants:** the queue to move. It never does.
- **Hides:** he doesn't know what the papers say either. He stamps them because stamping is the only thing that makes them real, and he's terrified that one day it won't.

### The thread-tender
Keeps the Thread-House warm. Sits at the end of the row, not in it.
- **Says:** "Warm enough?" and means *is it working*.
- **Wants:** the threads to stay local. She's the one who keeps them from reaching the village, and nobody asked her to.
- **Hides:** she talked to someone on the other side of the glass, once. She can't decide if it was the first arrival or a stranger from further out. She deleted it. She thinks about it every day.
- **Tell:** checks the glass with the back of her hand when she's lying.
- **Hook:** she'll show a stranger the deleted message for a promise not to tell the village.

### The name-broker
Runs the Market of Borrowed Names. Sells reputations by the day.
- **Says:** your new name, and then uses it like it was always yours.
- **Wants:** a name of her own. She's worn other people's so long she can't remember hers.
- **Hides:** her real name is on a wall in the Office of Unread Papers, and she can't read it, so she can't take it back. This is the name rented too long.
- **Tell:** answers to anything.
- **Hook:** she'll give a stranger a name for free, if they'll read one word off a paper for her.

## The Old Ones

### Baba Yaga
The fixer. House on a tank track. Plate on her knee, audience in the glass.
- **Wants:** to never be forgotten. Attention is her food now.
- **Hides:** the videos are lonelier than they look. She has an audience of thousands and no one to have tea with. The deals she makes are partly a way to get people to sit at her table.
- **Tell:** checks the plate mid-sentence, like a nervous habit, then pretends she didn't.

### The leshy
Lord of the forest. Lost in it.
- **Wants:** home. The place the machine can't find.
- **Hides:** he is afraid. He gave up his instinct for a screen and now the forest is a stranger to him, and he suspects the forest knows.
- **Tell:** argues with his GPS out loud. Loses.

### The rusalka
The river and the lake. Drowned a grudge, not a person.
- **Wants:** an apology. Not to her. To whoever she was before.
- **Hides:** she is waiting for someone who died a century ago, and she knows it, and waiting is the only thing she has left.
- **Tell:** her reflection lags a half-second behind her.

### The kikimora
The swamp. House-proud, petty, dangerous if disrespected.
- **Wants:** a proper house. The bog is a compromise and she knows it.
- **Hides:** she was a person once, a bad housekeeper who let a home go to ruin, and the swamp is her punishment and her address.

## The First Arrival
Not in the cast the village knows. Evidence of them is everywhere: the shrine, the upgraded house-legs, the words nobody uses right. `[?]` Are they dead, absorbed, or still here wearing a name. I left it open. It's the world's oldest question and its best hook.
