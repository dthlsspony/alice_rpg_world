## The frame
- The world is a Russian fairytale that's been left running a long time. Not a theme-park version: the real thing, with the parts that used to scare children still in it.
- Modern things leak in. Not people, not buildings. Little habits of the new world showing up where they shouldn't.
- **Nobody's eyes yet.** You cut the arrival and then said it plainly: world first, people later. So no hero walks through these pages. We write the place while it's empty of him. Whose eyes we borrow, if anyone's, is a question for after the ground holds.

## The rules that actually run things
The load-bearing ones. If these are wrong, the world falls over.
- **Hospitality is a real force.** Bread and salt given freely binds both sides. A guest who takes is a guest who owes. Not politeness, but rules.
- **Thresholds matter.** What crosses a doorway with permission is not the same as what crosses without.
- **Magic is in the air.** Not knowledge you carry, a part of everything. How good one is at magic is very personal.
- **[?]** "Very personal" could mean everyone touches it a little, or only some are born able. Which one? It changes whether magic is weather or a gift.

## Old magic and the new leak
- Old magic is folk-shaped. It works through deals, bargains, and rules, not spells. You don't cast it, you enter it, and then you're inside the terms.
- The new leak is electricity with no wires. It hums. It charges things that shouldn't charge. It's the one piece of our world that made it across.
- **[?]** You asked if the leak is technology intertwined with magic. If yes, then the hum isn't foreign, it's old magic that learned a new trick. That flips who owns it, and who's allowed to tend it.

## The hum
- Past the last house there's a field, and the field hums. Low, constant, like a held note too deep to name.
- It charges. Slowly, one percent at a time. A dead phone in a pocket comes back to life out here.
- Whoever built the field either never needed to charge anyone for it, or is long gone. Tesla's dream died because nobody could figure out how to bill for it. This field has no bill.
- **[?]** You said there should be something *in* the field making the electricity. What shape? A stone, a tree, a half-buried machine, a creature? Whatever it is, someone probably tends it, or used to, and that person is a lead.

## Places
- **The village.** Main gates are open. People look normal, but something is off. The dress looks normal until it doesn't, the writing is off, the boots have wooden parts. You notice the details before you can name what's wrong.
- **The tavern.** Where the language lives. Strangers, jokes, gossip, the day's news. No sign outside; you find it by smell and laughter.
- **The field.** See the hum.
- **[?]** Is there a city? Still open. A world that misremembers writing might still hold one, badly.

## People
- **Humans**, ordinary, living the way their grandparents did, because nothing here forgets how.
- **The old ones.** Leshy, domovoi, rusalka and the rest, as neighbors, not monsters. They keep their own rules and mostly keep to themselves.
- **Politics.** The old ones have their borders. One old one for a house, a forest, or a lake. Territory is a person here, not a line on a map.
- **[?]** What happens when two old ones share a border, or a human crosses one without asking? That's probably where the first real conflict of any scenario lives.

## The chief
- He is big, red and loud. A man you could see from afar and run away from to save your ears from busting, but overall he is a good man.
- **[?]** How does a man like that end up chief? "Whoever remembers the most" still feels right for a world where memory is the only ledger, but you'd know better than me whether that fits him.

## What I deliberately left out
- A map. No map, no borders; we find them by walking.
- A magic system with numbers. No spell lists, no costs table. It's rules and bargains or it's nothing.
- A chosen one. There isn't one.
- A main hero. Not yet, by your call. People come after the world does.
- Clean edges. If this reads too tidy, it stops being a place you can walk into.
