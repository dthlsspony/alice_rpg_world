# The Village

One street. Forty-odd souls. It sits where the maintained road stops pretending to be a road.

## The shape of it
- Houses face the street like they're listening. Painted shutters, carved lintels, and a different knot tied over every door (household magic, see `../systems/magic.md`). The knots aren't decoration. They're a lock.
- The **writing is off**. A sign has the right shape and the wrong letters. Ask one villager to read it and they'll tell you, confidently. Ask the next and they'll tell you something else. Nobody finds this strange. Writing here is a polite fiction, and everyone plays along.
- **Nobody leaves without saying goodbye out loud.** Not a custom. A rule. Slip out quiet and something follows you a while, patient, until you turn around and say it to the empty road.

## Places inside
### The gates
Stand open. No wall worth the name, no guard. The gate's only job is the goodbye. There's a post beside it with a fresh-cut top, like someone maintains the leaving too.

### The tavern
No sign outside. You find it by the smell of stew and the sound of laughter through a door that looks like any other. This is where the language lives: strangers, jokes, gossip, the day's news. The one room in the village that is honest by accident.

### The chief's long house
The biggest building on the street, painted red, loud even when it's empty. Smoke always going. A domovoi lives behind the stove and predates the village (see `../people/npcs.md`). The chief holds court on a bench by the door, because a man that big doesn't fit in the back room.

### The banya
The bathhouse, out behind the houses. Threshold magic is thickest here: heat, steam, nakedness, and a spirit that is not always friendly. You go in clean and you come out clean, or you don't come out. Never enter on a day you broke a promise.

### The well
Where the gossip is. Also where the village drops things it wants to forget. It is deeper than a well this old should be. `[?]` What's at the bottom is a hole I left: I keep wanting it to be the first arrival, but that might be too neat.

### The edge of the field
Where the houses stop, the hum starts (see `../locations/points-of-interest.md`). The village pretends the field is just a field. At night the children aren't allowed past the last fence, and the fence is newer than the rule.

### The old stones
The graveyard. Carved names, worn past reading, which in this village means nothing because nothing here reads anyway. The oldest stones aren't human-shaped. The old ones are buried here too, or something is.

## What's off here
The tells. Use them to build unease before anyone names it:
- The writing that everyone reads differently.
- Boots with wooden parts, sewn in, not cobbled.
- A door that opens for you a half-second before you touch it.
- Clocks that all disagree and are all correct.
- A child who answers a question you only thought.

## Who's here
The chief, the fool, the tavern keeper, the debt keeper, the domovoi. See `../people/npcs.md`.
