# Points of Interest

The places that aren't a village or a city but pull a scenario toward them.

## The field
Past the last house of the village. It hums. Low, constant, a held note too deep to name. It charges things that shouldn't charge: a dead phone comes back one percent at a time out here. Nobody bills for it. Its maker is the fool, and the fool doesn't think in bills. See `../people/npcs.md` and `../systems/modern-leak.md`.

Hook: the field is safe by day and honest by night. What you say standing in it, it remembers. `[?]` Does it remember for you, or against you.

## Baba Yaga's house
On the far side of the forest, past the swamp. The house walks. Not on chicken legs: on a tank track, a metal analogue someone upgraded in, with a transform mode that folds the legs flat for rolling. Baba Yaga is a **fixer**, not a villain. People come to her with problems that have no clean solution, and she solves them with terms.

She has a **plate**: a slab of glass she talks to. Far away, in the city and beyond, people watch. She is the world's only working internet personality, and she built an audience the old way, one deal at a time. Her hidden thought: the audience is insurance. A woman who is watched is a woman who is not forgotten. See `../people/secrets.md`.

Hook: she'll fix anything, and the price is never money. It's a favour, a story, or an introduction.

## The shrine of the earlier arrivals
A stake by the field with a dead phone lashed to the top, screen cracked, and a ring of small offerings at its foot. The hero wasn't the first to come across. Others came, and one of them left this. The phone gets a bar near the field. Sometimes, rarely, it rings. Nobody has ever answered it. `[?]` Who's calling is the biggest open question in the world, and I left it that way on purpose.

## The barrow
A burial mound on the forest edge, older than the village, older than the road. The old ones call it *the one who won't lie down*. Grass won't grow on one side of it. Dig into it and you'll find the dead don't want to be a find. The dead here keep their grudges.

## The mill
Between village and river. It grinds grain by day and something else by night. The miller's family has run it for six generations and none of them sleep facing the wheel. A good place to hide, a bad place to rest.

## The lake
Still, black, and patient. The rusalka's larger house. Fish it and you catch what you lost, not what you wanted. Swim at your own risk; the lake keeps what it holds.
