# The Forest Paths

Between the village and the city. The leshy's country. The paths rearrange when nobody is looking. Except one.

## The maintained road
The road out of the village toward the city. Someone keeps it: a fresh post, a cleared ditch, a boot-worn track. Hope with an author. Follow it and you reach the city, eventually. The maintenance is a person, not a policy. `[?]` Who, is a hole. My lean: the chief, quietly, because a road that ends is a road that lets people leave, and he wants them to come back.

## The path that gives up
Branches off the maintained road and stops, mid-forest, at nothing. Locals say it used to go somewhere. The forest ate the somewhere. Walk to its end and you'll find a worn track that simply isn't there anymore, and the trees watching you decide what to do about it.

## The crossroads stone
Three roads, one stone. Carvings too worn to read, and in this world reading is a joke anyway. The crossing is a deal. Leave something on the stone or take something from it, and either way you're inside the terms now. Take twice and it takes back with interest.

## The river crossing
Rusalka water. Do not answer if the water calls you by name. Pay the crossing: something small, given freely, said out loud. Refuse and she takes something you didn't offer. See `../people/npcs.md`.

## The swamp
Kikimora country. Petty, house-proud, guarding a house that is a bog. She'll trade directions for a scrap of something domestic: a needle, a rag, a bit of thread. Lie to her and the path she gave you loops back to her, and she's laughing.

## The old oak
The leshy's seat, the heart of the forest, or what's left of it. See below.

## The leshy, and his GPS
He used to be the one who moved the paths. Travellers got lost because he wanted them to. Now he has a GPS, and it moves *him*, and it's wrong, and he is lost in his own forest. He stops travellers and asks for directions to places that are inside him: the spring he was born from, the tree that holds his name, the way home. He doesn't believe their answers. The machine disagrees with them.
