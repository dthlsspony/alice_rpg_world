# The City

The village's opposite number, downstream, where the leak pooled and thickened. The city **reads**. That's the whole difference. Where the village misremembers writing, the city writes everything down and remembers it wrong on purpose.

Its real name is carved over the Copper Gate. Nobody agrees how to read it. So locals call it the City, and that stuck.

## Districts
### The Copper Gate
Old wall, new hinges. The hinges hum. A guard checks papers that nobody can read, nods, and lets you through. The nod is the actual check.

### The Market of Borrowed Names
You can rent a reputation here. Wear a name that isn't yours for a day and the city treats you accordingly. Return it late and it starts to fit. `[?]` What happens to a name that's rented too long is a hook I left open.

### The Thread-House
Where the internet is. Warm, humming, full of glass. People sit in rows before slabs of it, talking to no one, and somewhere far away someone listens. The threads are local. They don't reach the village. That's the point of the village.

### The Office of Unread Papers
The bureaucracy. It issues documents. The documents are binding and illegible, both at once. Stamped, filed, and unreadable. The Office is the closest thing this world has to a written law, and it's built on the fact that no one can check it.

### The Tower that Hums
The grid. Electricity wired like a nervous system, metered, sold. The field's wild hum, tamed and billed. The tower is where the leak became infrastructure. Old ones who embedded tech into their lives all draw from here, whether they admit it or not.

### The voivode's court
The city lord's hall. Bigger than the chief's house and quieter, which tells you which man is actually feared.

## Who's here
The voivode, his daughter, the knight of his guard, a great many clerks, the thread-tender at the Thread-House, and the name-broker at the Market. See `../people/npcs.md`.

## What's off here
- Paper that's warm to the touch.
- A queue that has been standing since before you arrived and will be standing after you leave.
- The hum under the floor, always, like a held breath.
- Names on doors that you read differently each time you pass.
